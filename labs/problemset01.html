Spatial Differences in Severe Income Loss During the 2022 Shanghai Lockdown

The question

Among respondents with a usable coordinate, was the share reporting a lockdown-related income reduction of more than 50% different between Shanghai's seven central districts and the other nine districts?

Before looking at the results, I expected the share reporting severe income loss to be higher in the Other 9 districts than in the Central 7 districts.

My proposed mechanism was that employment in some non-central districts may have depended more on work that could not continue remotely, making income more vulnerable to mobility restrictions and workplace closures. This was an expectation rather than a causal claim because the analysis does not directly measure occupation or ability to work remotely.

I would have reconsidered this expectation if the two groups had shown similar severe-income-loss shares, if the Central 7 had shown the higher share, or if reasonable sensitivity checks had reversed the direction of the difference.

The data

I used the 2022 Shanghai Lockdown Survey provided through SOCS-SHU 205. The survey was open to Chinese adults aged 18 or older who were living in Shanghai during the lockdown. Participants completed an online Chinese-language questionnaire through Wenjuan Xing between 29 April and 1 June 2022.

Sampling was purposive rather than random. Researchers aimed to recruit approximately 200 respondents from each of Shanghai's 16 districts, with oversampling to account for invalid responses. District respondent counts therefore reflect the sampling design rather than the actual population size of each district.

The original survey contained 3,348 respondents. Of these, 2,795 had usable geographic coordinates and could be included in the coordinate-based spatial analysis. The remaining 553 respondents were excluded because they could not be spatially assigned using coordinates.

The outcome variable, Reduction of your income, is coded: 0 = no income reduction, 1 = income reduction below 50%, and 2 = income reduction above 50%. I treated code 2 as severe income loss. Among the 2,795 located respondents, 994 reported an income reduction greater than 50%.

I also used longitude and latitude, the respondent-reported District field, and the coordinate-based district fields survey_code and name_en. I identified 245 cases in which the respondent-reported district disagreed with the coordinate-based district.

The Shanghai district polygons come from DataV.GeoAtlas. The boundary file has no stated licence, so I use it for derived maps and measurements rather than redistributing the original boundary file.

Detailed postal addresses had already been removed from the course-provided survey before I received it. Because the remaining coordinate information can still be sensitive, I do not publish the respondent-level survey file or individual respondent locations.

All percentages reported below are unweighted shares. They describe this purposive survey sample and should not be interpreted as representative population estimates for all Shanghai residents.

The method

Respondent points were first created from longitude and latitude in EPSG:4326 — WGS 84. The analysis layers were then prepared in EPSG:32651 — WGS 84 / UTM Zone 51N for spatial analysis.

I assigned each respondent to the Shanghai district polygon containing their coordinate using a point-in-polygon spatial join. All 2,795 respondents with usable coordinates received a coordinate-based district assignment.

I then created two comparison groups. The Central 7 consisted of Huangpu, Xuhui, Changning, Jing'an, Putuo, Hongkou, and Yangpu. The Other 9 consisted of Pudong New Area, Minhang, Baoshan, Jiading, Jinshan, Songjiang, Qingpu, Fengxian, and Chongming.

The coordinate-based grouping produced 1,081 Central 7 respondents and 1,714 Other 9 respondents.

Severe income loss was defined as Reduction of your income = 2, meaning an income reduction of more than 50%. For each group, I divided the number of severe-loss cases by the group denominator to calculate an unweighted share.

I compared the two shares using their percentage-point difference. I also calculated a rough uncertainty margin for each share using approximately two standard errors:

Rough margin = 2 × √[(share × (1 − share)) / n] × 100

For the district-level choropleth, I created a binary loss50 variable, coded 1 for severe income loss and 0 otherwise. In QGIS, I used Join Attributes by Location (Summary) with district polygons as the base layer and respondent points as the comparison layer.

I calculated the district fields n, share_pct, and moe_pts, then mapped share_pct in ArcGIS Online using a sequential purple ramp with five equal-interval classes. All 16 districts contained at least 20 respondents, so no district required suppression.

The map

The choropleth maps the share of surveyed respondents reporting an income reduction greater than 50%, not the raw number of respondents. Darker purple represents a higher severe-income-loss share.

What I found

Unweighted shares from the 2022 Shanghai Lockdown Survey, a purposive online sample: 2,795 of 3,348 respondents had a usable coordinate. Respondents were assigned to districts using a point-in-polygon spatial join. Severe income loss was 15.0 percentage points more common among respondents in the Other 9 districts than in the Central 7. The result remained in the same direction and at almost the same size under both sensitivity checks.

Main result

In the Central 7 districts, 285 of 1,081 respondents reported an income reduction greater than 50%, producing a severe-income-loss share of 26.4% with a rough margin of ±2.7 percentage points.

In the Other 9 districts, 709 of 1,714 respondents reported severe income loss, producing a share of 41.4% with a rough margin of ±2.4 percentage points.

The difference was therefore 41.4% − 26.4% = 15.0 percentage points. The approximate ranges were 23.7%–29.1% for the Central 7 and 39.0%–43.8% for the Other 9.

At the district level, severe-income-loss shares ranged from approximately 20% to 58%. This shows that the broad Central 7 versus Other 9 contrast also contains substantial variation among individual districts.

Sensitivity checks

I changed two analytical decisions one at a time while keeping the outcome and the Central 7 versus Other 9 comparison fixed.

Alternative 1 — respondent-reported district: replacing the coordinate-based district assignment with the respondent-reported District field produced 27.1% in the Central 7 and 41.8% in the Other 9, a difference of 14.7 percentage points.

Alternative 2 — remove shared coordinates: after dropping all 234 respondents located at shared coordinates, the Central 7 contained 939 respondents, with 253 severe-loss cases = 26.9%. The Other 9 contained 1,622 respondents, with 674 severe-loss cases = 41.6%. Using the underlying proportions, the difference was 14.6 percentage points.

The result therefore survived both sensitivity checks: 15.0 pp → 14.7 pp → 14.6 pp. The Other 9 remained higher than the Central 7 under both alternative specifications.

What this does not show

My analysis cannot see the 553 respondents without usable coordinates. It also cannot represent people outside the survey's eligible or reachable online population. Because recruitment was purposive and the estimates are unweighted, the results should not be generalized directly to all Shanghai residents.

Spatial assignment is also imperfect. The main analysis retained 234 respondents at shared coordinates, some of which may represent geocoder fallback locations rather than precise residential locations.

In addition, 245 respondents had a mismatch between their respondent-reported district and their coordinate-based district. The sensitivity checks suggest that these issues do not materially change the broad Central 7 versus Other 9 comparison, but they remain limitations of individual spatial assignment.

The >50% income-loss threshold, the Central 7 versus Other 9 grouping, and the five equal-interval choropleth classes are analytical choices. Different defensible choices could change how the spatial pattern is summarized or visualized.

The analysis also identifies an association rather than a causal relationship. It cannot establish that living outside the Central 7 caused severe income loss, and it does not control for occupation, baseline income, employment sector, or ability to work remotely.

A Shanghai social-services or emergency-assistance planner could use this result as evidence that severe economic disruption among surveyed respondents was spatially uneven and as a reason to investigate non-central districts more closely or collect more representative district-level data.

The result would not justify assigning assistance to individuals solely according to their district, claiming that district location caused income loss, or allocating citywide resources directly in proportion to these unweighted survey percentages.
