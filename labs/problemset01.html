Spatial Differences in Severe Income Loss During the 2022 Shanghai Lockdown

The question

Among respondents with a usable coordinate, was the share reporting a lockdown-related income reduction of more than 50% different between Shanghai's seven central districts and the other nine districts?

The data

I used the 2022 Shanghai Lockdown Survey provided for the course. The survey was open to Chinese adults aged 18 or older who were living in Shanghai during the lockdown and was completed online through Wenjuan Xing between 29 April and 1 June 2022.

The study used purposive rather than random sampling. Researchers aimed to recruit about 200 respondents from each of Shanghai's 16 districts, with oversampling to account for invalid responses. The district sample sizes therefore reflect the recruitment design rather than the actual population size of each district.

The original dataset contained 3,348 respondents. Of these, 2,795 had usable geographic coordinates and were retained for the spatial analysis. The remaining 553 respondents could not be included because they did not have usable coordinates.

I also used the Shanghai district boundary file supplied through the course. The district polygons come from DataV.GeoAtlas, but the boundary file has no stated licence.

Detailed postal addresses had already been removed from the survey before the course-provided file reached me. Because the remaining geographic coordinates can still contain sensitive location information, I do not publish respondent-level locations or the original survey CSV.

The method

I first created respondent point features from longitude and latitude in EPSG:4326 — WGS 84. The analysis layers were then prepared in EPSG:32651 — WGS 84 / UTM Zone 51N.

I used a point-in-polygon spatial join to assign each of the 2,795 respondents with usable coordinates to the Shanghai district containing their coordinate.

I then created two comparison groups. The Central 7 consisted of Huangpu, Xuhui, Changning, Jing'an, Putuo, Hongkou, and Yangpu, with 1,081 respondents. The Other 9 consisted of Pudong New Area, Minhang, Baoshan, Jiading, Jinshan, Songjiang, Qingpu, Fengxian, and Chongming, with 1,714 respondents.

Severe income loss was defined as Reduction of your income = 2, meaning an income reduction of more than 50%. For each group, I divided the number of respondents meeting this criterion by the total number of respondents in that group to calculate an unweighted share.

I compared the two shares using their percentage-point difference and calculated a rough uncertainty margin using approximately two standard errors:

Rough margin = 2 × √[(share × (1 − share)) / n] × 100

For the district-level map, I created a binary loss50 variable, coded 1 for severe income loss and 0 otherwise. I summarized this by district in QGIS and mapped the resulting share_pct field in ArcGIS Online using a sequential purple ramp with five equal-interval classes. The minimum reporting threshold was n = 20. All 16 districts met this threshold, so no district required suppression.

What I found

Declaration

Unweighted shares from the 2022 Shanghai Lockdown Survey, a purposive online sample: 2,795 of 3,348 respondents had a usable coordinate. Respondents were assigned to Shanghai districts using a point-in-polygon spatial join in QGIS, and the Central 7 and Other 9 groups were defined from those coordinate-based district assignments. QGIS was used to compute the spatial assignments and district-level shares, while ArcGIS Online was used to create the final choropleth map. The main difference of 15.0 percentage points remained very similar in both sensitivity checks—14.7 percentage points using respondent-reported districts and 14.6 percentage points after removing shared-coordinate respondents—so the result is reported as stable to these two analytical decisions.

In the Central 7 districts, 285 of 1,081 respondents reported an income reduction greater than 50%. This produced a severe-income-loss share of 26.4%, with a rough margin of ±2.7 percentage points.

In the Other 9 districts, 709 of 1,714 respondents reported an income reduction greater than 50%. This produced a share of 41.4%, with a rough margin of ±2.4 percentage points.

The difference between the two groups was therefore 15.0 percentage points. Among respondents in this survey, severe income loss was substantially more common in the Other 9 districts than in the Central 7 districts.

The district-level map also shows variation within the two broad groups, with district severe-income-loss shares ranging from approximately 20% to 58%.

What this does not show

My analysis cannot see the 553 respondents without usable coordinates. It also cannot represent people outside the survey's eligible or reachable online population. Because recruitment was purposive and the estimates are unweighted, the findings describe the survey respondents rather than all Shanghai residents.

Spatial assignment is also imperfect. The main analysis retained 234 respondents at shared coordinates, some of which may represent geocoder fallback locations rather than precise residential locations. In addition, 245 respondents had a mismatch between their respondent-reported district and their coordinate-based district.

I tested both decisions in the sensitivity analysis. Using the respondent-reported district instead of the coordinate-based district produced a difference of 14.7 percentage points. Removing all 234 shared-coordinate respondents produced a difference of 14.6 percentage points.

The result therefore survived both sensitivity checks: 15.0 pp → 14.7 pp → 14.6 pp. The Other 9 remained higher than the Central 7 in both alternative analyses.

The analysis shows a spatial association rather than a causal relationship. It cannot establish that district location caused severe income loss, and it does not control for factors such as occupation, income, employment sector, or ability to work remotely.

The >50% income-loss threshold, the Central 7 versus Other 9 grouping, and the five equal-interval map classes are also analytical choices that influence how the pattern is summarized and visualized.
